# Scholarly sources and why they matter

These are published scholarly books that provide **context and methodological framing**. They do not independently verify the 60 CSV rows; those must be checked against the primary text.

1. **El-Zein, Amira. (2009). *Islam, Arabs, and the Intelligent World of the Jinn*. Syracuse University Press.** https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1j5d836  
   This book studies jinn across religious, literary, and folkloric contexts. It helps explain why a jinn should not be reduced to a single fixed role such as 'monster' or 'helper.' I will use it to question the categories in my dataset rather than assume all communities understand jinn the same way.

2. **Campbell, Joseph. (1949). *The Hero with a Thousand Faces*. Pantheon Books.** Smithsonian catalogue: https://www.si.edu/object/hero-thousand-faces%3Asiris_sil_15227  
   Campbell is useful as an example of a broad comparative approach to myth and recurring narrative patterns. It also gives me a framework to question: searching for similarities across stories can hide differences in their particular historical and cultural settings. This is a comparison framework, not proof that every culture shares the same meaning.

3. **Horta, Paulo Lemos, ed. (2021). *The Annotated Arabian Nights: Tales from 1001 Nights*. Translated by Yasmine Seale. Liveright.** Editor's description: https://www.paulolemoshorta.com/books/annotated-arabian-nights  
   This annotated edition foregrounds translation and the history of which tales entered different versions of *The Arabian Nights*. It is relevant because my CSV uses Andrew Lang's English adaptation. The source helps me ask how editors, translators, and the movement of stories between languages affect what becomes available to classify.

## Primary text (not counted as a scholarly secondary source)

**Lang, Andrew, ed. (1898). *The Arabian Nights Entertainments*.** Project Gutenberg edition: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/128/old/128-h/128-h.htm . This is the literary text from which the initial events were coded. It should be cited for the story content, not treated as a modern scholarly interpretation.
